# 8. PART F — DESIGN FOR AWARD-LEVEL STORYTELLING

Treat storytelling as a product interaction, not just marketing copy.

## 8.1 Create a narrative arc

Develop a coherent visitor journey with an opening question, discovery, explanation and meaningful conclusion.

A possible structure:

**ACT I — Wonder**

Establish the Moon as a real environment with unfamiliar observational constraints.

**ACT II — The Question**

Why can two nearby lunar locations experience different visibility conditions?

**ACT III — Investigation**

Invite the user to inspect actual sites, select time and compare evidence.

**ACT IV — Discovery**

Show genuine observational differences or scientifically meaningful similarities.

**ACT V — Explanation**

Reveal how geometry, observer assumptions and validated terrain information affect interpretation.

**ACT VI — Understanding**

Leave users with an accurate understanding of why this information matters and what remains uncertain.

Refine this structure based on the actual scientific data.

Do not make every visitor sit through a mandatory cinematic sequence.

## 8.2 Two complementary experience modes

Design:

**Guided Discovery**

A short, beautiful, curated introduction that helps first-time visitors experience a meaningful scientific observation.

**Open Exploration**

A fast, direct workspace for users who want to investigate independently.

These must share one authoritative scientific data foundation.

The narrative experience should not contain fabricated results or a separate mock scientific simulation.

## 8.3 Develop actual storytelling artifacts

Produce:

- Primary headline and alternative headline concepts.
- Hero subtitle.
- Introductory narrative.
- Section transitions.
- Interactive prompts.
- Scientific annotation style.
- Concise explanatory language.
- Discovery-state text.
- Final conclusion and call to action.

Avoid inflated language such as "revolutionizing lunar exploration."

Favor precise, memorable scientific communication.

## 8.4 Judge attention and demonstration quality

Evaluate the experience at four points:

- First 5 seconds: Is it immediately distinctive?
- First 15 seconds: Does the visitor understand the central question?
- First 60 seconds: Can the visitor complete a meaningful interaction?
- First 3 minutes: Can the visitor understand the evidence and limits?

These are evaluation goals, not observed usability results unless tested.

Develop a genuinely persuasive demonstration narrative that shows the product working.

Verify the current official demo format and duration requirements before producing final competition materials.
